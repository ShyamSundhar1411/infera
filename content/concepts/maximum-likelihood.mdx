## The idea

You have some data and a family of models, such as normal curves with different centres and spreads. Each choice of parameters says how likely every data point is. Multiply those numbers together and you get the likelihood: how probable the whole dataset is under that choice.

Maximum likelihood picks the parameters with the largest likelihood. For a normal curve that turns out to be the sample mean and the spread of the points around it. For a coin it is the share of heads. Results you could have guessed, but now with a principle behind them that also works where guessing does not.

## Likelihood is not probability

The probability $P(\text{data} \mid \theta)$ is a function of the data with the parameters fixed. The likelihood $L(\theta)$ is the same expression read the other way: the data is fixed and the parameters vary. It does not add up to 1 over $\theta$, and it is not the probability that the parameters are right. It only ranks parameter values by how well they explain what you saw.

## Why logs?

Multiplying a dozen densities gives tiny numbers, and multiplying thousands underflows to zero in a computer. Taking logs turns the product into a sum, keeps the numbers readable, and has its peak in exactly the same place because the logarithm only ever goes up.

## Reading the picture

Each data point sits on the axis with a stick up to the curve: the stick's height is that point's likelihood. A narrow curve makes the central sticks tall but leaves points in the tails with almost nothing, and one tiny stick drags the whole product down. A wide curve gives every point a short stick. The best curve balances the two.

The square map shows the log-likelihood for every combination of centre and spread, darker where it is higher. Your curve is the coral dot, the maximum is the teal ring, and clicking the map moves you there.

## The maths

<Formulas>

<Formula label="Likelihood">

$$
L(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i \mid \theta)
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Log-likelihood">

$$
\ell(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log f(x_i \mid \theta)
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Normal model">

$$
\hat{\mu} = \bar{x}, \qquad \hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \bar{x})^2
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Coin">

$$
L(p) = p^{h} (1 - p)^{n - h}, \qquad \hat{p} = \frac{h}{n}
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Finding the peak">

$$
\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial \theta} = 0
$$

</Formula>

</Formulas>
