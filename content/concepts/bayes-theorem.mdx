## The idea

Imagine a disease that 1 in 100 people have, and a test that catches 99% of real cases but also flags 5% of healthy people. You test positive. Are you probably sick? Most people say yes. The real answer is about 17%.

The trick is that healthy people vastly outnumber sick ones. Even a small false-positive rate applied to a huge healthy group produces more positives than the true cases do. Bayes' theorem makes that bookkeeping exact.

## How to think about it

1. Start with the prior: how common is the condition before you look at any evidence?
2. Ask how likely the evidence is if the condition is true (the sensitivity) and if it is false (the false-positive rate).
3. Count everyone who would see this evidence: true positives plus false positives.
4. The posterior is the share of that group who really have the condition.
5. Got more evidence? Today's posterior becomes tomorrow's prior.

## Reading the picture

The grid is 1,000 people. Coral squares are sick, the rest are healthy. Solid squares tested positive. Look only at the solid squares: the fraction that are coral is exactly P(sick | positive).

## Try this

1. Leave the defaults and read P(sick | positive). Then raise **Prevalence** to 20% and watch the same test become far more convincing.
2. Push **Specificity** to 99.9%. False positives nearly vanish, and a positive result becomes strong evidence even for a rare disease.
3. Set **Positive tests in a row** to 2. Each extra positive multiplies the odds by the likelihood ratio, so belief climbs fast.
4. Make the disease very rare (0.1%). Even an excellent test gives mostly false alarms. This is why screening programmes confirm with a second test.

## The maths

<Formulas>

<Formula label="Bayes' theorem">

$$
P(A | B) = P(B | A) · P(A) / P(B)
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Total probability">

$$
P(B) = P(B | A) P(A) + P(B | ¬A) P(¬A)
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Medical test">

$$
P(D | +) = sens · prev / (sens · prev + (1 − spec)(1 − prev))
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Odds form">

$$
posterior odds = prior odds × likelihood ratio
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Likelihood ratio">

$$
LR₊ = sensitivity / (1 − specificity)
$$

</Formula>

</Formulas>
