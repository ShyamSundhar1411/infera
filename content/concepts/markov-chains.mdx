## The idea

Suppose the weather has three moods: sunny, cloudy and rainy. A sunny day is followed by another sunny day 70% of the time, a cloudy one 20% of the time and rain 10% of the time. Cloudy and rainy days have their own rules. Crucially, tomorrow depends only on today, not on last week. That is the Markov property, or memorylessness.

Follow the chain day after day and something useful happens. The share of days spent in each state settles down to fixed numbers, and those numbers are the same whether you started on a sunny day or a rainy one. With the default arrows, about 46% of days are sunny, 31% cloudy and 23% rainy in the long run. That limit is the stationary distribution.

## How to think about it

1. List the states, and for each one the probability of moving to every other state next. These numbers form the transition matrix $P$, one row per current state.
2. Every row adds up to 1, because the chain has to go somewhere.
3. To get the chances in $n$ steps, multiply the starting distribution by $P$ a total of $n$ times.
4. The stationary distribution $\pi$ is the one that does not change when you take another step: $\pi P = \pi$.
5. If every state can reach every other and the chain does not cycle in lockstep, it forgets its starting point and converges to $\pi$.

## Where it shows up

**PageRank.** Imagine a web surfer who clicks a random link on the current page and, now and then, jumps to a random page instead. The pages are the states, and the share of time the surfer spends on each page is its rank. In the **Web pages** preset, page D has no links pointing to it, so it is only ever reached by a random jump and ends up with about 4% of the surfer's time.

**MCMC.** [Markov chain Monte Carlo](/concepts/mcmc/) turns this idea around. Instead of being handed a chain and asking for its stationary distribution, you design a chain whose stationary distribution is the posterior you want, then run it and collect the states it visits.

## The maths

<Formulas>

<Formula label="Markov property">

$$
P(X_{t+1} = j \mid X_t = i, X_{t-1}, \ldots, X_0) = P(X_{t+1} = j \mid X_t = i) = P_{ij}
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Distribution after n steps">

$$
\mu_n = \mu_0 P^n
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Stationary distribution">

$$
\pi P = \pi, \qquad \sum_i \pi_i = 1
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Long-run time share">

$$
\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbf{1}[X_t = i] \to \pi_i
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Weather chain, default arrows">

$$
\pi = \left(\tfrac{6}{13},\ \tfrac{4}{13},\ \tfrac{3}{13}\right) \approx (0.46,\ 0.31,\ 0.23)
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="PageRank with damping d">

$$
P_{ij} = \frac{1 - d}{n} + d \cdot \frac{\mathbf{1}[i \to j]}{\text{out}(i)}
$$

</Formula>

</Formulas>
