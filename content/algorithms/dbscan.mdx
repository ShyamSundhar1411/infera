## The idea

Picture a crowd at a festival seen from above. Groups of friends stand close together; a few people wander alone. If you can walk from person to person in small steps without crossing empty ground, those people belong to the same group.

Two settings define "close": the radius $\varepsilon$ and the minimum number of neighbours inside it. A point with enough neighbours is a core point. Clusters grow outwards from core points, and anything unreachable is noise.

## How it works

1. For each point, count the points within radius $\varepsilon$ (including itself).
2. Mark points with at least Min neighbours as core points.
3. Start a cluster at an unvisited core point and add everything within $\varepsilon$ of it.
4. Keep expanding through any core points you reach.
5. Points reached but not core are border points; points never reached are noise.

## Reading the picture

Each cluster gets its own colour, and noise points are shown in grey. The stats count clusters, core points and noise points.

Turn on Show $\varepsilon$ around core points to see the circle each core point uses. Overlapping circles chain into one cluster; a gap wider than $\varepsilon$ splits clusters apart.

## Try this

1. On **Moons**, use the defaults. Both crescents are found perfectly, something k-means cannot do.
2. Shrink Radius ($\varepsilon$) to 0.05. Clusters fragment and most points turn grey.
3. Grow Radius ($\varepsilon$) to 0.3. The gap between the moons gets bridged and everything merges into one cluster.
4. On **Circles**, turn on Show $\varepsilon$ around core points and tune Radius ($\varepsilon$) until the ring and the centre separate.

## The maths

<Formulas>

<Formula label="Neighbourhood">

$$
N_\varepsilon(p) = \{\, q : \lVert p - q \rVert \le \varepsilon \,\}
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Core point">

$$
\lvert N_\varepsilon(p) \rvert \ge \text{minPts}
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Reachable">

$$
q \text{ is reachable from } p \text{ if a chain of core points links them, each step } \le \varepsilon
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Noise">

$$
\text{points not reachable from any core point}
$$

</Formula>

</Formulas>

## Strengths and weaknesses

<ProsCons>
<Card>

### Good at

- Finds clusters of any shape.
- No need to choose the number of clusters.
- Flags outliers as noise automatically.

</Card>
<Card>

### Struggles with

- Struggles when clusters have different densities.
- Sensitive to $\varepsilon$, which depends on the data's scale.
- Distance becomes less meaningful in high dimensions.

</Card>
</ProsCons>
