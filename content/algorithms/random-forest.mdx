## The idea

Ask one person to guess the number of sweets in a jar and they may be way off. Ask a hundred people and average the guesses, and you are usually close. That only works if people make different mistakes.

A forest forces its trees to disagree in two ways. Each tree may see a random resample of the data, and at each split it is only allowed to consider one randomly chosen feature ($x$ or $y$). Diverse trees, averaged, give a steadier answer than any one of them.

## How it learns

1. For each tree, draw a bootstrap sample: $n$ points picked with replacement.
2. Grow a decision tree on that sample using Gini impurity.
3. At every split, pick $x$ or $y$ at random and find the best cut on that feature only.
4. Repeat for the chosen number of trees.
5. To predict, average the class probabilities from all trees.

## Reading the picture

The background blends the votes of every tree. Strong colour means the trees agree; pale bands show where they split their votes. The boundary is still built from axis-aligned pieces, but it is far smoother than a single tree's.

With Bootstrap samples on, about a third of the points are left out of each tree. Out-of-bag accuracy scores each point using only the trees that never saw it, which gives a fair test score for free.

## Try this

1. On **Moons**, set Number of trees to 1. You see one jagged tree. Slide it up to 60 and watch the boundary smooth out.
2. Compare Training accuracy with Out-of-bag accuracy. Training is near perfect; out-of-bag tells you what to actually expect.
3. Turn off Bootstrap samples. Trees now differ only in their random feature choices, so they agree more and the shading becomes harder-edged.
4. Try **Spiral** with Max depth 12. Forests can follow complex shapes that would make a single tree overfit badly.

## The maths

<Formulas>

<Formula label="Bootstrap">

$$
D_t = n \text{ draws with replacement from } D
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Left out">

$$
P(\text{point} \notin D_t) = \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)^n \approx \frac{1}{e} \approx 37\%
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Prediction">

$$
P(k \mid x) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_t P_t(k \mid x)
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Out-of-bag">

$$
\text{score } x_i \text{ with only the trees where } x_i \notin D_t
$$

</Formula>

</Formulas>

## Strengths and weaknesses

<ProsCons>
<Card>

### Good at

- Accurate out of the box with little tuning.
- Hard to overfit by adding more trees.
- Out-of-bag accuracy gives built-in validation.

</Card>
<Card>

### Struggles with

- Loses the readability of a single tree.
- Many trees cost memory and prediction time.
- Boundaries remain axis-aligned underneath.

</Card>
</ProsCons>
