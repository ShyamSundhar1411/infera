summary: "Logistic regression gives each class a linear score and turns those scores into probabilities. It learns the scores by gradient descent, making the true class more likely at every step."
---


## The idea

Think of each class as a candidate in an election, with a score that rises as you move in its favoured direction across the plane. Softmax converts the scores into vote shares that add up to one. Where two candidates tie, you get the boundary.

On its own the score is linear, so boundaries are straight lines. Feed it extra features like $x^2$, $xy$ and $y^2$, and the same linear model can draw circles, ellipses and curves in the original plane.

## How it learns

1. Start with all weights at zero, so every class has equal probability everywhere.
2. Compute each point's predicted probabilities with softmax.
3. Measure the log loss: how surprised the model is by the true labels.
4. Compute the gradient over all points at once and step the weights downhill by the learning rate.
5. Repeat until the gradient is tiny or the epoch limit is reached.

## Reading the picture

The background colour shows the most likely class, and its strength shows confidence. Pale bands mark places where the model is unsure. The boundary line is where two classes are equally likely.

Press play to watch the boundary rotate and slide into place. Log loss in the stats should fall steadily; if it bounces around, the learning rate is too high.

## The maths

<Formulas>

<Formula label="Features">

$$
\phi(x) = [1, x_1, x_2, x_1^2, x_1 x_2, x_2^2, \dots]
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Scores">

$$
z_k = w_k^\top \phi(x)
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Softmax">

$$
P(y = k \mid x) = \frac{e^{z_k}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Loss">

$$
L = -\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \log P(y_i \mid x_i) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_k \lVert w_k\rVert^2 \quad (\text{bias not penalised})
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Gradient step">

$$
w_k \leftarrow w_k - \eta \left[ \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \big(p_{ik} - \mathbf{1}[y_i = k]\big)\,\phi(x_i) + \lambda w_k \right]
$$

</Formula>

</Formulas>

## Strengths and weaknesses

<ProsCons>
<Card>

### Good at

- Outputs calibrated-looking probabilities, not just labels.
- Weights are interpretable and training is stable.
- Feature expansion makes it surprisingly flexible.

</Card>
<Card>

### Struggles with

- Linear in its features: curves need hand-made features.
- High feature degrees overfit without regularisation.
- Gradient descent can be slow with a poor learning rate.

</Card>
</ProsCons>
