## The idea

Each neuron is a tiny logistic-regression-like unit: it draws a soft line and reports which side you are on. The next layer combines those lines into corners and bands, and the layer after that combines those into blobs and spirals.

Nobody tells the network which lines to draw. It starts with random weights and keeps adjusting them in whatever direction reduces its error, a bit like sculpting by many small chisel taps.

## How it learns

1. Initialise the weights randomly.
2. Forward pass: push every point through the layers to get class probabilities from a softmax.
3. Measure the cross-entropy loss against the true labels.
4. Backward pass: use the chain rule to find how much each weight contributed to the loss.
5. Update every weight with Adam, which adapts the step size per weight. Repeat.

## Reading the picture

The background shows the predicted class at every point, shaded by confidence. Press play and watch it evolve from a vague tilt into a shape that wraps around the data.

The stats track epoch, loss, accuracy and the number of parameters. A falling loss with a frozen boundary means the network is just becoming more confident about what it already knows.

## Try this

1. On **Moons** with the defaults, press play. The boundary curves around both moons within a few hundred epochs.
2. Load **Spiral**, set Hidden layers to 3 and Neurons per layer to 16. Spirals need depth; compare with 1 layer of 4 neurons.
3. Switch Activation to ReLU. Boundaries become piecewise straight, with visible corners, instead of smooth tanh curves.
4. Set Weight decay ($\lambda$) high on a noisy **Moons**. The boundary stays smoother and refuses to chase individual points.

## The maths

<Formulas>

<Formula label="Layer">

$$
h^{(\ell)} = g\big(W^{(\ell)} h^{(\ell-1)} + b^{(\ell)}\big)
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Activations">

$$
\tanh(z), \quad \operatorname{ReLU}(z) = \max(0, z), \quad \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Output">

$$
P(y = k \mid x) = \operatorname{softmax}\big(W^{(L)} h^{(L-1)} + b^{(L)}\big)_k
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Loss">

$$
L = -\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \log P(y_i \mid x_i) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\sum \lVert W\rVert^2
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Adam">

$$
\begin{aligned}
&m \leftarrow \beta_1 m + (1-\beta_1)\,g, \quad v \leftarrow \beta_2 v + (1-\beta_2)\,g^2 \\
&\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\,\frac{\hat{m}}{\sqrt{\hat{v}} + \varepsilon}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Formula>

</Formulas>

## From a model to a system

A trained network is one box in a bigger machine. In a language-model product it sits inside a loop of retrieval, tools and checks, and most of the engineering is in that loop rather than in the weights.

## Strengths and weaknesses

<ProsCons>
<Card>

### Good at

- Can learn almost any boundary given enough units.
- Learns its own features instead of needing hand-made ones.
- Scales to images, audio and text.

</Card>
<Card>

### Struggles with

- Many hyperparameters and random starts to manage.
- Hard to interpret what each weight means.
- Overfits small datasets without regularisation.

</Card>
</ProsCons>
