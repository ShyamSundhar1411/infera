## The story

Flip the same biased coin $n$ times and count the heads. Each flip is a $\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ trial, the flips do not influence one another, and the total can be anything from 0 to $n$. The probability of exactly $k$ successes is $\binom{n}{k} p^k (1 - p)^{n-k}$: the chance of one particular arrangement, multiplied by the number of ways to arrange $k$ successes among $n$ slots.

On average you get $np$ successes, with variance $np(1 - p)$. When $p = 0.5$ the distribution is perfectly symmetric; push $p$ towards 0 or 1 and it leans against the nearest wall. As $n$ grows the bars settle into a familiar bell shape, which is why the Normal approximation works so well for large $n$.

## Formulas

<Formulas>

<Formula label="Support">

$$
k \in \{0, 1, \ldots, n\}
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="PMF">

$$
P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1 - p)^{n-k}
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="CDF">

$$
F(k) = \sum_{i=0}^{k} \binom{n}{i} p^i (1 - p)^{n-i}
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Expectation">

$$
\mathbb{E}[X] = np
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Variance">

$$
\operatorname{Var}(X) = np(1 - p)
$$

</Formula>

<Formula label="Mode">

$$
\lfloor (n + 1)p \rfloor
$$

</Formula>

</Formulas>

## Parameters

| Parameter | What it controls |
| --- | --- |
| $n$ | The number of independent trials; $X$ can take any whole value from 0 to $n$. |
| $p$ | The probability of success on each trial. It sets the centre ($np$) and, together with $n$, the spread $np(1 - p)$. |

## Where it shows up

- The number of heads in 20 coin tosses.
- How many of 50 surveyed people say they will vote for a given party.
- The number of defective items in a batch of 30, if each is faulty independently with probability $p$.
- How many of $n$ emails in an A/B test are opened.

## Try this

1. Set $n = 50$ and $p = 0.5$, then slowly lower $p$ to 0.05. Watch the symmetric bell become a right-skewed pile squashed against zero.
2. Compare $n = 50$, $p = 0.06$ with the Poisson page at $\lambda = 3$: the two sets of bars are almost identical, because $np = 3$ in both.
3. With $n = 50$ and $p = 0.5$, shade the interval from 22 to 28 (roughly the mean $\pm 1\sigma$, since $\sigma \approx 3.54$), then press **Draw 1000** and check that about that share of the histogram falls inside it.

## Related distributions

- A Binomial with $n = 1$ is the Bernoulli, and $\text{Binomial}(n, p)$ is the sum of $n$ independent $\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ variables.
- The Poisson is the limit of the Binomial as $n \to \infty$ and $p \to 0$ with $np = \lambda$ held fixed.
- For large $n$ (a common rule of thumb is $np$ and $n(1 - p)$ both at least 10) it is well approximated by a Normal with mean $np$ and variance $np(1 - p)$.
