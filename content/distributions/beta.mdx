## The story

Suppose you want to express what you believe about a coin's bias, a click-through rate or a conversion rate: some number between 0 and 1 that you do not know exactly. The Beta(α, β) family can be flat, U-shaped, humped in the middle or piled up against either end, all controlled by two parameters. Its mean is α/(α + β).

A useful way to read the parameters is as pseudo-counts: α behaves like a number of successes and β like a number of failures. Start with a Beta(α, β) belief about p, observe s successes and f failures, and your updated belief is exactly Beta(α + s, β + f). The larger α + β, the more concentrated the curve, just as more data makes you more certain.

## Parameters

| Parameter | What it controls |
| --- | --- |
| α | Pulls probability towards 1; think of it as prior successes. Raising α shifts the mean α/(α + β) upwards. |
| β | Pulls probability towards 0; think of it as prior failures. Raising both α and β together narrows the curve without moving its centre much. |

## Where it shows up

- Uncertainty about a website's conversion rate in Bayesian A/B testing.
- The proportion of a day a machine spends idle.
- A batter's underlying hit rate, estimated from a season of at-bats.
- The fraction of a task completed by a deadline in project planning.

## Try this

1. Set α = β = 1 to see a flat line, then lower both to 0.5. The curve becomes U-shaped: values near 0 and 1 are now more likely than values in the middle.
2. Play Bayesian updating: start at α = 1, β = 1, then imagine 7 successes and 3 failures by setting α = 8, β = 4. The mean moves to 8/12 ≈ 0.667 and the curve narrows. Shade from 0.5 to 1 to see how confident you now are that p exceeds a half.
3. Compare α = β = 2 with α = β = 10. Both are centred on 0.5, but the standard deviation falls from about 0.224 to about 0.109. Press **Draw 1000** on each to see the difference in the histogram.

## Related distributions

- Beta(1, 1) is the Uniform on [0, 1].
- It is the conjugate prior for the p of a Bernoulli or Binomial: a Beta prior plus Binomial data gives a Beta posterior.
- The j-th smallest of n independent Uniform(0, 1) draws follows Beta(j, n − j + 1).
- If X is Gamma(α, θ) and Y is Gamma(β, θ), independent, then X/(X + Y) is Beta(α, β).
