## The story

One Exponential wait is the time until the next event; add up k independent Exponential waits, each with mean θ, and you get the time until the k-th event. That sum is Gamma(k, θ). The definition extends smoothly to non-integer k, which makes the Gamma a general-purpose model for positive, skewed quantities.

The mean is kθ and the variance is kθ². The shape k controls the form: below 1 the density shoots up towards infinity at zero, at exactly 1 it is the Exponential, and above 1 it rises to a peak at (k − 1)θ before tailing off. As k grows, the skew fades and the curve looks more and more Normal. The scale θ simply stretches the horizontal axis.

## Parameters

| Parameter | What it controls |
| --- | --- |
| k | The shape: roughly, how many Exponential waits are being added up. Larger k means a less skewed, more bell-like curve. |
| θ | The scale: the mean of each underlying Exponential wait. Doubling θ doubles the mean and quadruples the variance. |

## Where it shows up

- The total time to serve the next three customers in a queue.
- Monthly rainfall totals, which are positive and right-skewed.
- Insurance claim sizes.
- A prior for a Poisson rate or a Normal precision in Bayesian statistics.

## Try this

1. Fix θ = 1 and sweep k from 0.5 to 10. Watch the curve go from a spike at zero, through the Exponential at k = 1, to an almost symmetric hump with its mode at k − 1.
2. Set k = 1.5 and θ = 2, then open the Chi-squared page with k = 3. The two curves are the same, because Chi-squared(3) is Gamma(3/2, 2).
3. Choose k = 2, θ = 1.5, press **Draw 1000** and check the sample mean against kθ = 3 and the sample variance against kθ² = 4.5.

## Related distributions

- The Exponential with rate λ is Gamma with k = 1 and θ = 1/λ.
- Chi-squared with k degrees of freedom is Gamma(k/2, 2).
- Independent Gammas with the same θ add: Gamma(k₁, θ) + Gamma(k₂, θ) is Gamma(k₁ + k₂, θ).
- For large k it is approximately Normal with mean kθ and variance kθ².
