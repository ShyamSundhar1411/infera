summary: "The waiting time until the next event when events arrive at random at a constant rate λ."

## The story

If events arrive as a Poisson process with rate $\lambda$ per unit time, the gap until the next one is Exponential. Its density $\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$ is highest at zero and decays steadily, so short waits are common and long waits are rare but always possible. The mean wait is $1/\lambda$ and the standard deviation is also $1/\lambda$.

Its signature property is memorylessness: $P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = P(X > t)$. If a bulb with an Exponential lifetime has already lasted 1000 hours, its remaining life has the same distribution as a brand-new bulb's. It is the only continuous distribution with this property.

## Parameters

| Parameter | What it controls |
| --- | --- |
| $\lambda$ | The rate: the average number of events per unit time. A higher rate means shorter waits, with mean $1/\lambda$ and variance $1/\lambda^2$. |

## Where it shows up

- Time between customers arriving at a shop counter.
- Time until the next radioactive decay in a sample.
- The lifetime of an electronic component that does not wear out.
- Time between requests hitting a web server.

## Try this

1. Shade from 0 to the mean $1/\lambda$. Whatever $\lambda$ you choose, the probability is $1 - e^{-1} \approx 0.632$, so most waits are shorter than average: the long tail drags the mean upwards.
2. Set $\lambda = 0.5$ and press **Draw 1000**. Check that the sample mean is close to 2 and the sample variance close to 4, the square of the mean.
3. Switch to **Sample means** with $n = 2$, then $n = 30$. The averages stop piling up at zero and form a bell around $1/\lambda$: the Central Limit Theorem straightens out even a strongly skewed shape.

## Related distributions

- The Exponential with rate $\lambda$ is the Gamma with $k = 1$ and $\theta = 1/\lambda$.
- It gives the gaps between events in a Poisson process, whose counts in a window of length $t$ are $\text{Poisson}(\lambda t)$.
- Chi-squared with $k = 2$ is the Exponential with rate $1/2$ (mean 2).
- It is the continuous analogue of the Geometric: both are memoryless waiting times.
